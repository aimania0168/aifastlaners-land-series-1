Be honest for a second

AI made it easy to feel productive.

A new tool every week. A folder of saved prompts. Courses you’re halfway through. It feels like progress, and it’s comfortable, because consuming never asks you to risk anything.

But nobody pays you for what you’ve watched or what you know about. They pay for what you’ve made that solves their problem. That gap, between consuming and producing, is the entire game.

Three roads

Where you spend your time decides where you end up.

MJ DeMarco’s The Millionaire Fastlane describes three ways people move through money. AI doesn’t change the map. It changes how cheaply you can start building on the fastest road.

Lane 01

Sidewalk

No plan. Whatever you earn gets spent, and the future is someone else’s problem.

Lane 02

Slowlane

Lane 03

Fastlane

The filter

Five questions to ask before you build anything.

The Fastlane book gives a framework for judging a business idea, known as CENTS. Here’s how I’d point it at AI. If an idea fails these, no amount of clever prompting will save it.

C

Control: do you own it?

If one platform can change the rules or delete your account overnight, you’re renting, not building.

E

Entry: what’s your edge once starting is easy?

If AI makes it simple for you to begin, it makes it simple for everyone. Easy to enter means crowded.

N

Need: is someone already looking for this?

A cool demo is not a need. A real problem, with someone willing to pay to solve it, is.

T

Time: does it need you every hour?

Automate the repeatable parts and keep your judgment for the parts that need it. Don’t build yourself a new job.

S

Scale: can it serve 1,000 as easily as 10?

This is where AI earns its keep: the same effort reaching far more people.
